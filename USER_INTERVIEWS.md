## Interview 1 — The “Power User” CSE Student

**Profile:**  
3rd-year Computer Science Engineering student. Heavy AI user for coding, debugging, side projects, and technical research.

**Interview Format:** Discord call  
**Date:** 2026-05-22  
**Duration:** ~15 minutes  

### Q: Which AI tools do you currently pay for?

**A:**  
"I pay for Cursor Pro and ChatGPT Plus. I used to have GitHub Copilot, but once I started using Cursor heavily, Copilot felt redundant."

### Q: Roughly how much do you spend per month?

**A:**  
"About $40 a month. $20 for ChatGPT and $20 for Cursor."

### Q: What do you mainly use these tools for?

**A:**  
"Mostly coding and debugging. I also use ChatGPT to simplify difficult CS concepts and research topics."

### Q: Do you feel you're getting value for money?

**A:**  
"Definitely, but it also feels financially unsustainable as a student. Some months it feels painful paying for subscriptions."

### Q: Would you use a tool that audits AI subscriptions and spending?

**A:**  
"Absolutely. Especially if it could tell me whether I’m actually using enough features to justify premium plans."

### Q: What would make you trust a tool like that?

**A:**  
"It would need to be transparent about privacy and ideally open-source."

### Key Takeaways
- Students strongly depend on AI tools despite financial pressure
- Subscription overlap is common
- Transparency and trust matter heavily

## Interview 2 — The “Budget Hacker” CSE Student

**Profile:**  
2nd-year Computer Science Engineering student. Very cost-conscious and skeptical of recurring subscriptions.

**Interview Format:** WhatsApp chat  
**Date:** 2026-05-22  
**Duration:** ~10 minutes  

### Q: Which AI tools do you currently pay for?

**A:**  
"Technically none directly. Me and a few friends pool money into shared OpenAI and Anthropic API access."

### Q: Roughly how much do you spend per month?

**A:**  
"Usually around $5 to $7 depending on workload."

### Q: What do you mainly use these tools for?

**A:**  
"Debugging C++ code, fixing pointer errors, and proofreading lab reports."

### Q: Do you feel you're getting value for money?

**A:**  
"For the price, yes. But the overall experience is pretty bad because we’re sharing unofficial infrastructure."

### Q: Have you considered switching to another option?

**A:**  
"I’ve thought about going fully free, but free tiers become frustrating during heavy coursework weeks."

### Q: Would you use an AI spend optimization tool?

**A:**  
"Yeah, especially for people who subscribe to multiple AI products without realizing how much they’re spending."

### Q: What would make you trust a tool like that?

**A:**  
"It would need strong privacy messaging. If it asks for sensitive financial access immediately, I wouldn’t trust it."

### Key Takeaways
- Users actively create workarounds to avoid official subscriptions
- Cost matters more than premium UX for many students
- Trust and privacy concerns strongly affect adoption

## Interview 3 — The CSE Associate Professor

**Profile:**  
Associate Professor in Computer Science Engineering. Uses AI for academic research and writing while remaining highly concerned about ethics and privacy.

**Interview Format:** Google Meet conversation  
**Date:** 2026-05-22  
**Duration:** ~20 minutes  

### Q: Which AI tools do you currently pay for?

**A:**  
"I personally pay for Claude Pro. The university provides Microsoft Copilot, but I find Claude much better for research reasoning."

### Q: Roughly how much do you spend per month?

**A:**  
"About $20 personally."

### Q: What do you mainly use these tools for?

**A:**  
"Research summarization, literature reviews, formatting citations, and improving grant proposal writing."

### Q: Do you feel you're getting value for money?

**A:**  
"It saves a significant amount of time, but I also feel conflicted about depending on AI in academic work."

### Q: Have you considered cancelling your subscription?

**A:**  
"Yes, mainly because of data privacy concerns around unpublished research."

### Q: Would you use a tool that helps track and optimize AI spending?

**A:**  
"I think many academics and students would benefit from something like that."

### Q: What would make you trust such a platform?

**A:**  
"A very clear privacy policy and transparency around how user data is processed."

### Key Takeaways
- Academic users care heavily about privacy and ethics
- Many users continue paying despite discomfort around AI dependency
- Even institutional AI access often fails to satisfy advanced users
